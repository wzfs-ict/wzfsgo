How this unit is tested

Every mark in Cambridge ICT belongs to one of three assessment objectives.

ObjectiveWhat it asks you to doCommand words it usesWhere it appears
AO1 RecallRemember and communicate ICT knowledgeName · State · Give · Identify · Describe · ExplainPaper 1 — 32% of the qualification
AO2 ApplyApply knowledge and skills to produce a solutionUse · Produce · Create · CompletePapers 2 and 3 — 60%
AO3 AnalyseAnalyse, evaluate, judge and concludeCompare · Discuss · Evaluate · Justify · RecommendPaper 1 — 8%
Unit 2 is a Paper 1 unit

Almost every Unit 2 mark is written theory. The examiner is testing whether you can name the right device for a situation and say WHY it suits that situation. Questions are nearly always set in a context — a supermarket, a bank, a greenhouse, a hospital — so a device name on its own is rarely the whole answer.

THE GOLDEN RULE — ONE SENTENCE PER MARK

The number of marks tells you how many separate points to make. [4 marks] means FOUR points, so write FOUR sentences. Three sentences cannot score four marks however good they are.

And never write a brand name. Cambridge states that no marks are awarded for brand names of software or hardware. Write “word processing software”, not the product name.

The questions that appear in this unit

The right-hand column is the wording that earns the mark.

Exam questionKey words that score
Describe how Optical Mark Recognition works. Give one example of its use. [3]detects the presence or absence of marks · in fixed positions on a form · light is shone and shaded positions reflect less light · example: multiple-choice answer sheets / lottery tickets
State two differences between OMR and OCR. [2]OMR reads only WHERE marks are · OCR recognises the SHAPE of characters and converts them to editable text
Give two advantages of RFID over barcodes. [2]no line of sight needed — read through packaging · many tags read at once · tag can be rewritten · more durable / harder to damage
Describe two differences between a laser printer and an inkjet printer. [4]laser uses toner powder and a drum, inkjet sprays liquid ink · laser is faster for long print runs · laser has a higher running cost to buy but lower cost per page · inkjet is cheaper to buy but ink runs out sooner
Explain why a dot matrix printer is used in a warehouse. [2]it is an impact printer so it can print multi-part / carbon-copy forms · it works in dusty or damp conditions where other printers fail
Describe how a 3D printer produces an object. [2]it builds the object up in thin layers · material is added one layer at a time until the solid object is formed
Explain the difference between a monitoring system and a control system. [2]monitoring only records and reports the readings · a control system compares readings with a stored value and sends signals to actuators to change the conditions
Explain two advantages of chip and PIN over a magnetic stripe card. [4]the chip stores encrypted data so it is far harder to clone · the PIN must be entered, so a stolen card cannot be used without it · a magnetic stripe can be copied with a simple reader
THE TRAPS IN THIS UNIT
  • OMR is not OCR. OMR detects WHERE marks are. OCR recognises WHAT the characters say. "OMR scans the paper" is describing OCR and scores nothing.
  • A touch screen is both. It is an input device when you press it and an output device when it displays. Say which one the question is about.
  • An actuator is not an output device on its own — it is the component that converts the signal into movement. Motors, buzzers, heaters and lamps are the devices it drives.
  • Do not answer a "which device" question with a brand or product name. Name the type of device.
3

AO2 — Application

Choosing the right device for a situation you have not seen before.

How to spot a scenario question

A scenario question gives you a setting — a supermarket, a bank, a greenhouse, a hospital, a library. The device facts are the same as always. What changes is that your answer must say why that device suits THAT setting. An answer that would fit any situation scores half marks.

The scenarioWhat it is really askingThe device you need
A supermarket reads product codes at a checkoutFast, automatic, no typingBarcode reader
A school marks 2,000 multiple-choice sheetsReads shaded positions in bulkOMR
A bank processes chequesReads the magnetic characters at the bottomMICR
Number plates are read at a car park barrierReads actual characters as textOCR
A warehouse tracks pallets through packagingNo line of sight, many at onceRFID
A greenhouse keeps temperature constantReads conditions, then acts on themTemperature sensor + microprocessor + actuator
A shop needs a customer to prove the card is theirsSecurity at the point of saleChip and PIN reader
An architect prints large-scale building plansAccurate line drawings on big paperPlotter
A factory prints multi-part delivery notes in a dusty bayImpact printing, carbon copiesDot matrix printer
THE AO2 SENTENCE FRAME — NAME IT, THEN TIE IT TO THE SCENARIO

“The most suitable device is ___ . This is because in a ___ the user needs to ___ , and ___ can ___ .”

Worked example: “The most suitable device is an RFID reader. This is because in a warehouse the pallets are wrapped in packaging, and RFID can be read without line of sight and can read many tags at once.”

4

AO3 — Analyse, Compare, Discuss, Recommend

Only 8% of the qualification, but the questions worth the most marks.

Command wordWhat you must produceThe trap
CompareSimilarities AND differences between two things.Writing about only one of them.
DiscussBoth sides — advantages AND disadvantages.Giving only advantages. A one-sided answer is capped at half marks.
EvaluateBoth sides, then a judgement on which is better.Stopping before you give the judgement.
JustifyA choice plus the reasons that support it.Stating the choice with no reason.
RecommendA clear decision, supported, tied to the scenario.Sitting on the fence — “both are good”.

Getting started — the T-chart method

Before writing a discuss or evaluate answer, spend 30 seconds drawing a T on your paper. Left side FOR, right side AGAINST. Write two points on each side. Now you have four points for a [4 mark] question and you cannot accidentally write a one-sided answer.

Modelled AO3 answer

“A supermarket is considering replacing its barcode system with RFID tags. Discuss whether it should do so. [6]”

RFID does not need line of sight, which means a whole trolley of items could be read at once instead of scanning each barcode separately. In addition, RFID tags are more durable than printed barcodes, which tear or smudge, so fewer items would fail to scan.

However, RFID tags cost considerably more per item than a printed barcode, which matters when a supermarket sells millions of low-value items. On the other hand — or rather, in addition — the existing checkout hardware and stock system would have to be replaced, which is a large one-off cost and requires staff retraining.

Overall, I would recommend that the supermarket keeps barcodes for everyday low-value stock and uses RFID only for high-value items, because the speed and durability gains do not justify the tag cost on cheap goods.

Notice the shape: two points FOR, two points AGAINST, then a recommendation tied to the situation. That is what earns the top level.

SENTENCE FRAMES FOR THIS QUESTION TYPE

One advantage of using a ________ is that ________ , which means ________ .

One disadvantage of using a ________ is that ________ , so ________ .

A ________ is suitable here because ________ . (scenario questions — NAME · SAY · TIE)

An advantage that would fit any device (“it is fast”) scores nothing on its own. Say WHY it is fast and what that gives the user in THIS situation.

SYLLABUS NOTE — MICR

MICR is no longer in the 0417 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Do not revise it and do not use it as an answer.

The direct data entry devices examined are: magnetic stripe reader, chip and PIN reader, RFID reader, OMR, OCR, bar code reader and QR scanner.
