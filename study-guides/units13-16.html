How to use this pack

These four sections are taught and examined as a block, so they share one pack. Sections 1–4 teach you how the marks are earned. Section 5 is every keyword you need, in English and 한국어, grouped by unit. Section 6 is the checklist to tick before the end-of-block test.

These are practical units. Most of their marks come from Papers 2 and 3, where you actually build the document or the chart. Paper 1 examines only the parts the syllabus lists as “know and understand” — the purpose of headers and footers, corporate house style, validation, and choosing a chart. Both are covered here.

THE GOLDEN RULE — ONE SENTENCE PER MARK

The number of marks tells you how many separate points to make. [4 marks] means FOUR points, so write FOUR sentences.

For these four units there is a second rule that costs just as many marks: read a practical instruction as a checklist. “A4, landscape, 2cm margins, two columns, 1.5cm gap” is FIVE separate marks. Tick each one off as you do it — miss one and you lose it even if everything else is perfect.

And never write a brand name. Name the type of software: “word processing software”, not the product.

The questions that appear across these four units

The right-hand column is the wording that earns the mark.

Exam questionKey words that score
State the purpose of a header and a footer. [2]content that repeats at the top / bottom of every page · so information such as page number, date or file name appears consistently without retyping it
Explain why a file path must be inserted as an automated field rather than typed. [2]an automated field proves the file was genuinely saved at that location · it updates itself if the file is moved or renamed, whereas typed text does not
Explain two benefits to an organisation of a corporate house style. [4]all documents look consistent · which makes the organisation look professional and aids brand recognition · staff save time because the formatting is decided already · fewer errors and less rework
Describe the advantage of applying a named style rather than formatting text manually. [3]a style applies every setting in one action · changing the style updates every paragraph using it · manual formatting cannot be updated globally and drifts out of consistency
Explain the difference between validation and verification. Give one example of each. [4]validation = the COMPUTER checks the data is reasonable, e.g. a range check · verification = checking the data was entered ACCURATELY, e.g. double entry or a visual check against the source
Describe two validation checks for a date of birth field. [4]format check — the date matches the pattern dd/mm/yyyy · range check — the year falls within sensible limits · presence check — the field is not left blank · each needs the name AND what it does
A spell checker has been run but errors remain. Explain why. [2]a spell checker only compares words with a dictionary · a correctly-spelled word used in the wrong place, such as "their" for "there", is not flagged
State the most suitable chart type and justify your choice. [3]name the type · pie = proportions of one whole · bar or column = comparing separate categories · line = change over time · the justification must refer to the data given
State four items that should be added to a chart to make it clear. [4]chart title · x-axis title · y-axis title including units · legend when there is more than one data series
Explain why pattern fills are used for a chart printed in black and white. [2]different colours become similar shades of grey · patterns stay distinguishable so the segments can still be told apart
THE TRAPS IN THESE FOUR UNITS
  • Validation is not verification. Validation is the computer deciding the data is sensible. Verification is checking it was typed accurately. Learn one clean sentence and one example for each — these are guaranteed marks.
  • A widow is at the TOP of a page, an orphan at the BOTTOM. A widow is the last line of a paragraph stranded at the top of the next page; an orphan is the first line stranded at the bottom of the previous one.
  • Copy a style name EXACTLY as the task gives it, capitals and hyphens included. "WZFS-Body" and "WZFS Body" are different names and the wrong one scores zero.
  • A legend is only needed when there is more than one data series. Adding one to a single-series chart is not wrong, but it is not one of the four marks either.
  • Do not include a totals row when you select data for a chart — it distorts the scale and loses the mark.

AO2 — Application

Choosing the right feature for a document you have not seen before.

How to spot a scenario question

A scenario gives you a setting — a company newsletter, a school report, a survey, a sales dataset. The features are the same as always. What changes is that your answer must say why that feature suits THAT document. An answer that would fit any document scores half marks.

THE AO2 SENTENCE FRAME — NAME IT, THEN TIE IT TO THE DOCUMENT

“The feature needed is ___ . This is because in this ___ the document must ___ , and ___ does that.”

Worked example: “The chart type needed is a line graph. This is because the data shows rainfall for each month across a year, and a line graph shows how a value changes over time.”

AO3 — Analyse, Compare, Discuss, Recommend

Only 8% of the qualification, but the questions worth the most marks.

Command wordWhat you must produceThe trap
CompareSimilarities AND differences between two things.Writing about only one of them.
DiscussBoth sides — advantages AND disadvantages.Giving only advantages. A one-sided answer is capped at half marks.
EvaluateBoth sides, then a judgement on which is better.Stopping before you give the judgement.
JustifyA choice plus the reasons that support it.Stating the choice with no reason.
RecommendA clear decision, supported, tied to the scenario.Sitting on the fence.

Getting started — the T-chart method

Before writing a discuss or evaluate answer, spend 30 seconds drawing a T on your paper. Left side FOR, right side AGAINST. Write two points on each side. Now you have four points for a [4 mark] question and you cannot accidentally write a one-sided answer.

Modelled AO3 answer

“A company is deciding whether to require all staff to use a corporate house style for every document. Discuss whether it should. [6]”

A house style means every document looks consistent, which makes the company appear professional and helps customers recognise its brand instantly. In addition, staff save time because the formatting decisions are already made, so they can concentrate on the content rather than the appearance.

However, a rigid house style can be unsuitable for documents with a different purpose, such as a poster or a presentation for children, where the standard fonts and colours may not work. On the other hand, staff need training before they can apply named styles correctly, and until then documents may be formatted manually and drift out of line anyway.

Overall, I would recommend that the company adopts a house style for all external documents, because brand consistency matters most where customers see it, while allowing some flexibility for internal or specialist material.

Notice the shape: two points FOR, two points AGAINST, then a recommendation tied to the situation.
